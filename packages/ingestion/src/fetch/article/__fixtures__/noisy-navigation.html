Cutting Through Page Furniture

Most of a modern article page is not the article. Navigation, consent banners, newsletter prompts, related-story rails, and analytics scaffolding routinely outweigh the prose by an order of magnitude, and a naive text extraction stores all of it as though it were content.

Readability heuristics exist precisely because the structural signal is unreliable. Scoring candidate containers by text density and link density recovers the main body far more often than any hand-written selector list, and it degrades gracefully on pages that were never designed with semantic markup in mind.

The parts that must be removed unconditionally are the ones that carry behaviour rather than meaning: scripts, styles, forms, and embeds. Stripping them is not a heuristic — it is the difference between storing a document and storing a program.

Broken Markup Still Has Words

Parsers meet documents that no validator would accept, and refusing to read them is rarely the right answer. Tag soup is the normal condition of the open web: unclosed paragraphs, attributes without quotes, headings that never terminate, and elements nested in orders the specification does not describe.

A forgiving DOM implementation recovers a usable tree from nearly all of it. What matters is that recovery is deterministic — the same bytes must produce the same tree and therefore the same stored Markdown and the same content hash, run after run, or deduplication becomes a coin flip.

The failure that does deserve a typed error is the page with no article in it at all. A navigation stub, a consent wall, or a client-rendered shell yields a handful of characters and should be reported as unreadable rather than stored as though it were prose.

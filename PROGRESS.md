## Decisions made

- **P1f `frontend`, run at Opus.** Last of the five Opus-run banks, per Justin's instruction to hold P1d through P1g at Opus before switching models for the app-build phases. No exemplar in the spec is a `frontend` question, so unlike `apis`, `testing`, `platform`, `typescript` and `cloud` there was nothing to include verbatim; E2 and E6 were matched for shape wherever code appears.
- **P1f `frontend`.** 34 questions, split 12 glossary / 22 depth, 24 mcq / 10 order (70.6% mcq). Glossary sits at the top of the spec's 10 to 12 band and depth sits just inside the 18 to 23 band. Difficulty is 12 at level 1, 13 at level 2, 9 at level 3.
- **P1f `frontend`. Code used generously, as the spec asks for this domain.** 5 of the 34 questions carry a `code` block, all in the depth section: `frontend-013` (server data copied into component state via an effect), `frontend-015` (a bare `invalidateQueries()` call with no key), `frontend-016` (every-twentieth-sample downsampling of a force trace), `frontend-019` (a memo wrapper defeated by an inline mapped array and an inline arrow), `frontend-022` (a container element used as a button). Each has a `stemSpoken` so nothing code-shaped reaches kokoro.
- **P1f `frontend`. Dense data in the browser is the weighted sub-theme**, per the phase brief and the prep doc's "large datasets in the browser (near certain)". Ten questions sit on it: `frontend-002` and `frontend-020` (virtualisation and what it silently takes away), `frontend-003`, `frontend-011`, `frontend-016` and `frontend-026` (downsampling, largest triangle three buckets, and why every-Nth sampling destroys peak force), `frontend-017` and `frontend-018` (vector versus canvas, in both directions: why vector dies at fifty thousand points, and what canvas costs you in hit testing, keyboard access and a text alternative), `frontend-025` and `frontend-028` (diagnosing and ordering performance work).
- **P1f `frontend`. Canvas is covered as a two-sided trade, not as the answer.** `frontend-017` asks why vector graphics struggle and `frontend-018` immediately asks what you owe once you move to canvas. That pairing is deliberate: the phase brief and the prep doc both frame this as a trade-off question, and a candidate who only says "canvas is faster" has answered half of it.
- **P1f `frontend`. Server state versus client state carries the TanStack Query weight.** Eight questions: `frontend-001` (the distinction itself), `frontend-004`, `frontend-005`, `frontend-006` (stale-while-revalidate, cache keys, deduplication), `frontend-013` (the effect-plus-useState anti-pattern with code), `frontend-014` and `frontend-027` (optimistic updates, including the cancel step people leave out), `frontend-015` (invalidation scope, with code), `frontend-029` (the stale-while-revalidate lifecycle as an ordering).
- **P1f `frontend`. Accessibility is framed as one discipline with testing, per the spec.** `frontend-021` makes the point explicitly (a role query reads the same tree a screen reader reads, so the failing test _is_ the accessibility bug), and `frontend-012`, `frontend-018`, `frontend-020`, `frontend-022`, `frontend-023` and `frontend-030` carry it through accessible names, canvas, virtualised row counts, a container used as a button, and focus management on both sides of a dialog.
- **P1f `frontend`.** `frontend-034` (replacing a hand-rolled fetching hook across a large front end) is a deliberate `platform` crossover in the same spirit as `typescript-031`: pick a screen people already complain about, prove it, agree the convention, codemod the rest, lint last. It rehearses the adoption ladder from `platform-012` in a front-end costume, which is the most likely shape of question given the platform-squad framing.
- **P1f `frontend`. Two ordering questions were reshaped before shipping, because their first form was arbitrary.** A "where should this state live" ladder (derived, local, lifted, URL, global store) was cut: the position of the URL against a global store is genuinely arguable, and the spec says to delete an arbitrary ordering rather than defend it. A form-submission ordering originally ended with two mutually exclusive branches (map server field errors / invalidate on success), so it was cut to four items ending at the success path and the server-error handling moved into the explanation (`frontend-033`).
- **P1f `frontend`.** Nine questions sit at difficulty 3. `distractorNotes` are filled on all six of those that are MCQs (`frontend-013`, `015`, `016`, `018`, `019`, `023`) as the spec requires, plus five difficulty-2 MCQs where a near-miss option needed naming. The other three difficulty-3 items are orderings (`frontend-027`, `031`, `034`) and carry `orderRationale` instead, per the schema.
- **P1f `frontend`.** `stemSpoken` / `explanationSpoken` added only where TTS would mangle the text, not blanket-applied: the five code-bearing stems, "largest triangle three buckets" written out in place of the initialism, and explanations containing colons introducing lists, camelCase identifiers or bracketed clauses.
